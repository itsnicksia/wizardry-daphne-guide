In an age before the continent was brought together as one, Arianaute was born within the high walls of a temple. A great temple it was, raised by Lord Fidelis, who willed that the god he served should be worshipped from one shore of the continent to the other, and within its halls dwelt clergy beyond counting, and these went forth into every land, zealous and unwearying in the spreading of their creed.

Her father was numbered among the high prelates of the Temple. A devout and austere man, he was stern with himself above all others, and for this the faithful revered him, so that with each passing year his station within the Temple rose higher. Her mother, desiring that Arianaute should become such a paragon as her father was, schooled the girl in hard disciplines, and spared her no severity.

Craving the elusive warmth of her parents' love, Arianaute surrendered her will to them with absolute, single-minded devotion. Day after unrelenting day, she pored over the holy canons, offered solemn supplications to the gods, and consumed her waking hours in the grim mastery of the sword, that she might be forged into a holy champion destined to purge the realm of both heretic and heathen.

"Cast out the evil of the profane world, and the world shall be made pure again..."
Nurtured upon the Temple's unyielding dogma, her spirit untouched by even a shadow of doubt concerning the divine justice preached by the prelates, Arianaute grew to womanhood, and in time, her destiny claimed her and she took her place within the Holy Legion-the formidable martial arm of the Grand Temple of Fidelis.

As one of the Holy Legion, Arianaute waged war against her god's sworn foes-vile heretics and the undead. When first she slew a man, her hands trembled, and when she stood against some foul and ravening monster, her legs shook beneath her. Yet Arianaute held fast to a creed in which she believed.

"I need no heart. I am the blade of my God."
And thus was Arianaute's sword honed to a cold, lethal perfection.

With every grim mission fulfilled, those around her lauded her-"Truly, she is the prelate's daughter"-and in time Arianaute came to be known as the Saintess of Condemnation. No foe, whatever its shape or horror, could any longer stir fear within her breast, and in the fervent certainty that she wrought justice, she came to know even a burning exultation.

Yet there came a dark hour during a march of condemnation when calamity befell her company. A band of heretics, festering with venomous malice toward Arianaute-whose name as the Saintess of Condemnation of the Grand Temple echoed across the realm-fell upon her host in a treacherous ambush.

A sudden stroke fell upon Arianaute, and she was sorely wounded. Even as the heretics pressed forward to administer the killing blow, her youthful squires-mere children, really-interposed their bodies, a living shield against the onslaught, only to be cut down, one after another, their innocent blood staining the forsaken soil.

So it was that, in those few bitter moments which the squires had bought with their young blood, the Legion managed to flee the field, bearing with them Arianaute, pale and broken, her life ebbing from her like a dark tide. Long did the priests chant over her in the shadowed halls of the Temple, and the physicians labored at her wounds with all their craft; yet in the silence of their hearts they believed she would never rise from that bed.

To the Grand Temple, that their revered saintess should succumb to vile heretics and perish was an insufferable catastrophe. To anchor Arianaute's fleeting life to the mortal realm, they resolved to employ a forbidden relic, wrested long ago from heathen cultists and sealed deep within the Temple vaults: the Holy Core of the Sage. It was said that whoever harbored this artifact within their flesh would gain an imperishable, deathless form-purchased only at the terrible price of unending torment.

With the dread artifact bound into her very marrow, Arianaute tore her way back to consciousness through a tide of blinding, white-hot agony. Not only was she restored to her former vitality, but she also gained capabilities that far transcended the fragile boundaries of mortalkind. Her spirit brimming with fervent gratitude toward her god and the uncanny potency of the Core, Arianaute swore an iron oath to press forward in her unyielding crusade for divine righteousness.

Returned from death in a miraculous resurrection, the saintess reaped an even more terrible harvest of victory upon the battlefield, driving the faithful into a fever of wild, fanatical zeal. Yet, in hushed tones, fearful voices whispered that she had become the very incarnation of condemnation.

Then came the fateful day, when Arianaute received yet another decree of judgment from the Archprelate. The Archprelate lamented, "Woe unto us, there is a heretic within the Temple who has used an accursed pagan artifact," and revealed the heretic's location to Arianaute. With a tense expression, Arianaute entered the designated room, accompanied by a small contingent of soldiers.

And there within that chamber, awaiting the blade of their god, were none other than her own mother and father.

A sick horror churned in Arianaute's breast, yet her fingers closed round the hilt of her sword with terrible, mechanical obedience and drew it from its sheath. For it was these very parents who had, from her childhood, forged her mind upon a single, unyielding dogma-that the decree of the Archprelate was divine law, absolute and unquestionable. Shrinking in terror before the cold steel wielded by the child of their own blood, her parents cried out in frantic, breathless desperation.
"It was to save your fleeting life that we wielded that accursed relic-and at the Archprelate's own command! If we are damned for it, then so are you, child-you are damned alongside us."

Then was Arianaute's soul riven, and a storm broke within her such that the very pillars of her faith seemed to crack. For, as her own parents had said, should they be condemned as sinners, so then should she, their daughter, be.

Seeing Arianaute struck senseless in her trance of despair, her parents desperately sought to spirit their child away from the chamber. But the men-at-arms moved with cold, unpitying speed, falling upon the wretched trio and binding all three in heavy iron.

The Temple delivered its uncompromising verdict, issuing a dreadful decree that proclaimed both the Saintess and her parents to be blasphemous heretics, condemned to suffer a common doom.

Surrounded by a throng of onlookers, Arianaute was led to the gallows to meet her end alongside her condemned parents. As the noose tightened and the platform gave way beneath her, her life force ebbed away into nothingness, leaving only a lifeless husk hanging before the silent, watchful crowd.

-Some days after the crowds had gone and the carrion birds had come, Arianaute awoke. The Holy Core of the Sage blazed in her breast like a heathen fire, and the noose bit deep into her throat. As she thrashed between these twin torments, the rope, rotted by rain and wind, parted at last, dropping her like a cast-off thing into the dust beneath the gallows.

Beneath the pale light of dawn, the throng assembled in the square stood paralyzed, spellbound witnesses to her unnatural resurrection. In the tumult that followed, Arianaute cut down the bodies of her mother and father, upon which corruption had already begun its work, and, gathering them into her arms, she passed from the sight of men, her face as empty as the eyes of the dead.

Forsaking the god she had once served, and bearing out of death a hatred that no grave could hold, Arianaute fell upon the Grand Temple of Fidelis again and again through the long procession of the years, and in all that time her hatred never cooled. And it is said that so terrible was her wrath that the once-mighty bastion of orthodoxy was bled white by her relentless fury, eventually falling into ruin.
